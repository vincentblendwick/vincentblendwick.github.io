Hybrid Training Club, and who keeps the pen

A gym in Stuttgart reopened after a rebuild: four training worlds, a coffee and shake bar, and almost no photos of itself. I took the brand onto every surface it touches and worked out the one tool their booking setup can’t give them. The hard part wasn’t making it look right. It was making it something the club can run without me.

THE CLUB’S INSTAGRAM AFTER THE REOPENING ⬩ ALL 88 MOTIFS IN 49 POSTS FROM THE PLAN

CONTEXT

Hybrid Training Club Stuttgart runs four training worlds under one roof — hybrid training, CrossFit, bootcamp and indoor cycling — plus a coffee and shake bar. In October 2026 it reopened after a rebuild.

The brand existed as a wordmark and a feeling. What was missing was everything around it: a social channel that sounds like the club, signage, menus, merch, and a way to keep all of it consistent when the people posting change.

A brand that only works while the designer is in the room isn’t a brand. It’s a dependency.

There was an operational gap too. The club agreed on eight visits per person per month with a corporate fitness partner, and the partner’s system can’t enforce that limit.

MY ROLE

Brand application and content system. Sole designer. The counting tool is designed and planned for 2027.

Shown with the owners’ permission — all of it.

NEXT ⬩ PLANNED FOR 2027

Eight visits a month, counted where the partner can’t.

Zählwerk will count check-ins per person and calendar month, split by training world and booking source. The limit is set per world and can be changed at any time. It reads the booking system’s export, and the columns are matched in the interface, because every export looks a little different. It is thought through, not built: development starts in 2027.

DECISIONS

Keep every post editable in the club’s own tool, even if it costs me control.

BECAUSE

A post the club can’t fix at seven in the morning is a post that waits for me. The brand has to survive a typo fix.

WHAT IT COSTS

Small changes happen without me. The rules have to be clear enough to hold anyway.

Write the rules down as rules, not as a mood.

BECAUSE

“Never shrink the logo” can be checked. “Keep it bold” can’t.

WHAT IT COSTS

A longer list, and every rule needs a reason the club agrees with.

Merch only from lines the club already says.

BECAUSE

A slogan nobody in the gym uses is a costume. A line from the warm-up is a souvenir.

WHAT IT COSTS

Fewer clever ideas. Every piece needs a source: a post, a sticker, a warm-up.

WHAT COULD GO WRONG

Someone new runs the channel, and the club slowly stops sounding like itself.

AUTHORITY

GUARDRAIL

RECOVERY

The owners approve. Facts come from the club, not from me.

Fixed rules, editable templates and one editorial plan that everyone works from.

Every post sits in the plan with its date and source, so drift shows up as a row, not as a feeling.
